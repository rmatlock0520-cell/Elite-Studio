What's inside

Five studios. One window.

Studio reads the files the game already writes (your journal, ship, cargo and key bindings), so it knows what you're flying and where you are without you typing a thing.

Studio Flight

Learn to fly with Flight Assist off

A trainer that flies like the game does with Flight Assist off: your ship's own turn rates, and momentum that keeps going until you stop it.

  • Nine exercises from Cadet to Elite: chase a target, fly a twisting tunnel, keep the nose on a moving dot.
  • Tune how your stick responds with Flight Assist off (your curves) using +/- buttons while you fly, and feel each change at once.
  • A guided stick check finds where your stick rests and sets your dead zones to match.
  • Take your curves into the game with Joystick Gremlin, or let Studio shape your stick itself through vJoy, a free virtual-joystick driver (install it, then bind Elite's axes to it). Turn on "Switch the curves when Flight Assist changes" in Flight's Options, and the game gets your Flight Assist off curves whenever FA is off.

Studio Market

The best trade for the ship you're in

Studio already knows your cargo space and jump range, so it skips straight to the good part.

  • Best hauling trades near you or anywhere, one way or round trip.
  • Find the nearest place that buys or sells a commodity, sells a module or a ship, or has a service.
  • See any station's price list, and what a ton makes between there and where you're docked.
  • The Sniffer checks your saved searches once an hour and plays a sound when a price hits your number.

Studio UI

Paint your HUD any colour you like

A theme editor for the cockpit HUD with a live preview, so you see the result before you take it into the game.

  • Works through the EDHM HUD mod, which Studio fetches for you from its makers' own page.
  • Save your themes and come back to them any time.

Studio Nav

Long trips, one jump at a time

Plot a route and Studio gives you one system at a time: copy its name, paste it into the galaxy map, jump. No more typing long star names.

  • Routes come from Spansh, the community's free route planner.
  • Looking for somewhere nobody has been? Studio helps you find unexplored space.

Studio Loadouts

Build it, engineer it, fly it

  • Your ship exactly as the game last reported it.
  • A ship designer for trying out new builds.
  • An engineering planner that turns a build into a shopping list.
  • Guardian unlocks, so you know what's left to grind.

Right in the cockpit

Press a key. There it is.

Overlays sit on top of the game. Press the key to show one and use it with the mouse; press it again to hide it. Each one remembers its own size and place.

F2Haulingbest trades
F3Findwho buys or sells
F4Routeyour next jump
F5Loadoutsyour ship
F6Flight inputsstick, throttle, buttons

The flight inputs overlay never takes your mouse. It's off until you turn on Overlay on/off in Flight's Options; then F6 shows it, and it can pop up by itself whenever Flight Assist is off.

Overlays are Windows-only for now, and show over Elite when it runs Borderless (Options › Graphics › Display › Window mode), not Fullscreen. Rather use other keys? You can change them in Studio Market's overlay settings.

Free play · no coins needed

Get Elite Studio

No account, no ads, nothing to pay. Updating is just running the newer setup; your settings and builds stay put.

Windows

Windows 10 (version 2004 or later) or Windows 11, 64-bit. Nothing else to install: .NET comes inside it, and the setup adds Microsoft's WebView2 Runtime if your PC lacks it.

Download for Windows

Linux & Steam Deck

For Elite played through Steam (Proton) on a Linux PC, or a Steam Deck in Desktop Mode. It has four of the five studios: UI, Market, Nav and Loadouts. Flight, the overlays and Nav scan are Windows-only for now.

On Linux you press F11 in the game yourself after saving HUD colours, and the HUD mod needs Proton 10 (not 11) for now. Steam Deck or an older Linux? Take the .flatpak; otherwise the .tar.gz.

Download for Linux
Heads up: the Windows installer isn't code-signed yet, so the first time you run it Windows may say "Windows protected your PC". Click More info, then Run anyway. Want to look first? Every line of the code is on GitHub.

Good questions

Before you launch

Is it safe? Why does Windows warn me?

Windows warns about any program that isn't signed with a paid certificate, and Studio isn't signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.

Studio is open source, so anyone can read exactly what it does on GitHub. If you use Windows 11's Smart App Control, it may block Studio until it's signed.

Does it change the game?

It reads the files the game writes (the journal, your ship, your cargo, your key bindings) and never touches the game's memory.

A few optional features do write: Studio UI installs the EDHM HUD mod into the game folder (downloaded from its makers) and saves your HUD colours there, and on Windows it presses F11 in the game to reload them. Studio Flight can shape your stick through vJoy if you turn that on.

Is it really free?

Yes. No account, no ads, no paid tier. It's open source under the GPL-3.0 licence, so it stays free.

Where do the prices and routes come from?

Market prices come from Ardent Insight, a free service by Iain Collins built on EDDN, the live data network other players' tools feed every time they dock. Stations, modules and ships come from Spansh. A price is as fresh as the last commander who docked there with one of those tools running.

Routes also come from Spansh, and exploration data from EDAstro: free services volunteers run for everyone. Studio only asks them when you search, and once an hour for each search you saved in the Sniffer.

Does it work on console?

No, sorry. Studio needs the files the PC version of the game writes, so it's for PC players on Windows, Linux or a Steam Deck.

Who makes it?

CMDR Saint, a player who wanted all of this in one window, built it with a lot of help from an AI coding assistant. Ideas and bug reports are always welcome on Discord or GitHub.
