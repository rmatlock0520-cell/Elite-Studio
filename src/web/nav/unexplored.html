This part is certain. With every scan the game says whether anyone had discovered or mapped that star, planet or moon before, and Studio counts the ones nobody had. "Before" means sold to the game's cartographers: your name goes on a find when you sell your exploration data, unless a commander who was there earlier sells first. Selling ends the trip, and your next jump starts a new one. Losing your ship loses its unsold data, so that starts a new trip too.

Where few have been

Nobody keeps a list of systems no one has visited. What there is, is the other side: a count of the systems commanders are known to have visited, sector by sector. Studio draws it as the galaxy seen from above and finds the nearest thinly known part in the direction you are flying.

The map is made from the sector list of EDAstro (edastro.com, by CMDR Orvidius), which Studio fetches only when you ask and keeps for a week. The system to head for is named by Spansh (spansh.co.uk). Both are free services run by players for all of us.

No map on this PC yet. Press "Get the map from EDAstro" on the left: Studio fetches one list of about 1.5 MB and draws the galaxy from above, shaded by how many systems are known in each part.

Thinly known: fewer than known systems in a square of 1,280 ly Better known, brighter with more You, and the way you are looking

Read it as "how busy", not "how finished". The galaxy has some 400 billion systems and about 100 million are known, so every square is mostly unexplored. A highlighted or dark square has had few visitors, and towards the rim it also has few stars; a bright one is well travelled, yet most of its stars are still nobody's. So you need not fly to a highlighted square to be first: a few hundred light years off the busy routes there are systems nobody has recorded almost anywhere, and the count on the left proves each one. The waypoint is for when you want a whole sector that few have seen.
